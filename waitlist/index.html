Why it's different

Three things other apps get wrong.

Lab-verified numbers. Personalized by AI.

Baseline nutrition comes from the USDA's FoodData Central, not crowd-sourced guesses. The real accuracy comes from the layer on top. An on-device model learns how YOU eat, detects which restaurant you're at from the photo, flags meals that don't match your normal day, and gets sharper every time you correct it. Static databases can't do that. Neither can plain AI. The combination is the product.

Private by default.

Recipes are generated by Apple's on-device Foundation Models. Pantry data never leaves your phone. No account required. Meal photos analyzed once, then forgotten.

Honest free tier.

Three AI scans a day and twenty pantry items are free, forever. Premium unlocks unlimited for $4.99 a month. No seven-day dark-pattern trial. No $80 wall just to see your macros.

FAQ

Questions people keep asking.

The product isn't live yet. Here's what's already decided, and what we're still figuring out.

When does SnapCal actually launch?

TestFlight invites start going out in early summer 2026. The full App Store launch follows a few weeks after beta feedback is incorporated. Waitlist members get the TestFlight invite before anyone else.

How accurate is the AI meal scanning?

Current internal testing targets a median calorie error under 15% against USDA-verified ground truth. Portion size is the hardest part, which is why every scan shows a confidence score and a slider to adjust. For packaged foods, a barcode scan is always more accurate than a photo.

Do I need to create an account?

No. All data lives in your iCloud by default, so your meals and pantry sync across your devices without us running a server that holds your information. Sign in with Apple is available if you want it, but not required.

What's actually in the free tier?

Three AI meal scans per day, unlimited manual logging, up to twenty pantry items, and one recipe suggestion per day. That's enough to use SnapCal seriously without paying. Premium at $4.99 per month unlocks unlimited scans, unlimited pantry, unlimited recipes, widgets, and the weekly shareable report.

How do items get into the pantry?

Four ways, fastest to slowest. One, snap your grocery receipt on the way out of the store and the whole haul populates in seconds, with expiration dates estimated by category. Two, barcode scan anything packaged. Three, voice: "two pounds of chicken, expires Friday." Four, manual add if you want to. The receipt path is the one most people will use most of the time.

Will there be an Android version?

Not at launch. Several differentiating features (on-device recipe generation, Live Activities, Vision-based scanning) are tied to Apple frameworks that don't have equivalents on Android. If the iOS version is successful we will revisit, but the honest answer is: probably not soon.

Does it sync with Apple Health?

Yes. Calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber, and water write to HealthKit with your permission. You can also read your Apple Watch activity into SnapCal to adjust daily targets based on what you've actually burned.

What if the AI gets my meal wrong?

Every scan shows a confidence score. You can adjust individual items, change portion size with a slider, or re-scan with a better angle. Manual entry from the USDA database is always one tap away, and repeated corrections help the app learn your defaults.
